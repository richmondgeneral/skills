# Carnival Glass Identification Reference

## Overview

Carnival glass is pressed glass with an iridescent surface treatment, produced primarily 1908-1930s (classic era), with reproductions and new production continuing today. Identification relies on pattern recognition, base shape, color, and iridescence quality rather than maker's marks—most carnival glass is unmarked.

## Major Manufacturers

### Northwood Glass Company (1908-1925)
**Location:** Wheeling, WV (later Indiana, PA)
**Mark:** "N" in circle (underside)—one of few marked carnival makers

| Good Luck | Horseshoe with "Good Luck," basketweave | Bowls, plates |
| Strawberry | Realistic strawberries and leaves | Bowls, plates |
| Hearts & Flowers | Hearts alternating with flowers | Bowls, plates, compotes |
| Three Fruits | Cherries, pears, apples | Bowls, plates |

| Singing Birds | Birds on branches | Water sets, mugs |

**Northwood Colors:** Marigold, amethyst/purple, green, blue, ice blue, ice green, white, aqua opal (rare)

## Color Identification Guide

**Base Colors (glass color before iridescence):**

| Color | Description | Rarity/Value |
|-------|-------------|--------------|
| Marigold | Clear glass + marigold iridescence | Most common |
| Amethyst/Purple | Purple glass | Common, popular |
| Blue | Various blue shades | Common to scarce |
| Green | Various green shades | Common to scarce |
| White | Milk glass base | Scarce |
| Ice Blue | Pale blue, almost clear | Scarce to rare |
| Ice Green | Pale green, almost clear | Scarce to rare |
| Red | True red (amberina process) | Rare, premium value |
| Aqua Opal | Aqua with opalescent | Rare |
| Vaseline | Uranium glass (glows UV) | Scarce |
| Smoke | Grayish | Uncommon |
| Teal | Blue-green | Uncommon |
| Lime Green | Yellow-green | Scarce |
| Peach Opal | Peach with opal edges | Dugan specialty |

**Iridescence Types:**
- **Marigold/Pumpkin:** Gold/orange—most common
- **Radium:** Mirror-like, chrome—Millersburg specialty
- **Electric:** Bright, multicolored—premium
- **Pastel:** Subtle, delicate—on light-colored glass
- **Satin:** Matte, frosty—less desirable usually

## Attribution Without Marks

When identifying unmarked carnival glass:

1. **Pattern first** - Match to known pattern; many are maker-exclusive
2. **Base shape** - Collar base, spatula feet, dome foot each suggest makers
3. **Color** - Some colors exclusive to certain makers
4. **Iridescence quality** - Radium = likely Millersburg
5. **Edge treatment** - Peach opal edges = likely Dugan
6. **Glass quality** - Weight, clarity, mold sharpness

## Common Reproductions & New Carnival

**Reproduction red flags:**
- Colors not made originally (red in patterns that weren't made in red)
- "Too perfect" iridescence
- Modern Fenton marks on old-style pieces
- Lighter weight than originals
- Different base configurations

**Known reproduction sources:**
- Fenton (1970s-2000s, usually marked)
- L.G. Wright (1960s-1990s, unmarked)
- Summit Art Glass
- Various imports (Taiwan, China)

**Key differences:**
- Original carnival has heavier, denser glass
- Original iridescence more subtle, varied
- Original mold detail sharper
- Reproduction colors often "off"

## Quick ID by Pattern Shape

**Water sets** (pitcher + tumblers): Northwood Grape & Cable, Fenton Orange Tree, Imperial Tiger Lily
**Punch sets:** Imperial Fashion, Millersburg Hobstar & Feather, Northwood Grape & Cable
**Bowls (ruffled):** Most common form—ID by interior pattern
**Plates:** Same patterns as bowls, flat form more valuable
**Vases:** Dugan Pulled Loop, Imperial Ripple common; swung vases valued by height

## Pricing Research Sources

When validating valuations, check these sources:

**Primary (recent sold data):**
- eBay "Sold" listings (filter last 90 days)
- LiveAuctioneers completed auctions
- WorthPoint (subscription, comprehensive)

**Secondary (reference/guide):**
- Standard Encyclopedia of Carnival Glass (Edwards/Carwile)
- Carnival glass collector club newsletters
- Specialized carnival glass auction houses

**Market context:**
- Carnival glass market has softened from 1990s-2000s peak
- Common patterns/colors have declined most
- Rare pieces, Millersburg, and unusual colors hold value better
- Condition increasingly important as supply exceeds demand
