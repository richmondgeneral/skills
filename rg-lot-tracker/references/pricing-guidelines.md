## Pricing Psychology

### Price Point Formatting
- Under $20: use `.50` increments (e.g., $8.50, $13.50, $19.50)
- $20–99: use whole-dollar pricing (e.g., $25.00, $35.00, $85.00)
- $100+: whole-dollar pricing only (e.g., $125.00, $150.00)
- Avoid `.99` and `.95` endings; use clean, intentional price points
- Keep two-decimal numeric values in Square/Whatnot payloads for API/platform compatibility

### Anchoring
- Group similar items at different price points
- Showcase premium items to make mid-range seem reasonable
- "Compare at" pricing works well on Square for perceived value

## Research Sources

### Sold Listings (Most Reliable)
1. **eBay Sold** — Filter "Sold items" in search. Best volume data.
2. **Worthpoint** — Historical auction data (subscription required)
3. **LiveAuctioneers** — Auction house results, good for high-value pieces

### Listed Prices (Less Reliable — these show asking, not selling)
- eBay current listings (useful for seeing competition)
- Etsy (often inflated 30–50%)
- Ruby Lane (high-end market, prices skew high)

### Condition Adjustment
- Research prices for comparable condition
- Adjust ±20–30% for condition differences
- Note if research comps were better/worse condition than your item

## Time-Based Pricing Strategy

### New Listings (Day 1–30)
- Price at researched value
- Allow 30 days at full price before considering adjustments

### Aging (Day 30–60)
- Consider 10–15% reduction
- Add to sale or clearance grouping
- Refresh listing description/photos

### Stale (Day 60+)
- Evaluate: reduce further, bundle with other items, or hold for right buyer
- Some items are worth waiting for (rare, seasonal, collectible)
- Consider if shelf space cost outweighs holding value

## Red Flags: Priced Too Low

Watch for these signals:
- Sells within hours of listing
- Multiple inquiries immediately
- Buyer seems overly eager

**When in doubt:** Price higher. You can always negotiate down or reduce later.
Better to leave room than leave money on the table. Rare items especially —
research more before committing to a price.

## Square-Specific Notes

### Payment Links
- Square processing: 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction
- Factor this into margin calculations
- "Free shipping" built into price vs. separate — decide per item

### Discounts
- Use Square's built-in discount features for multi-buy deals
- Track discount impact on margins in lot tracking
