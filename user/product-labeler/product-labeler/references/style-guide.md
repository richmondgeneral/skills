# Richmond General Style Guide for Product Listings

## Core Principles

### Customer-Facing Language Only
- ❌ NO wholesale terminology ("stock up", "keep your shelves stacked", "fly off the shelves")
- ✅ Focus on customer experience and what makes the product special
- ✅ Use "you" to speak directly to the customer

### Imperial Units First
- Always lead with ounces (oz) or fluid ounces (fl oz)
- Include metric in parentheses: "1.2 oz (34g)" or "16.9 fl oz (500ml)"
- Round imperial conversions to one decimal place

## Product Name Format

**Structure:** `[Brand] [Flavor] [Imperial Size] ([Metric Size]) - [Country] Import`

**Examples:**
- Lay's Korean Honey Mustard 1.2oz (34g) - Taiwan Import
- Doritos Garlic Steak 1.4oz (40g) - Taiwan Import
- Fanta White Peach 16.9 fl oz (500ml) - China Import

## Description Structure

### Opening Hook
- Lead with the unique flavor or experience
- Use **bold** for key flavor descriptors
- Immediately establish exclusivity

### Cultural Context Section
**Explain why this flavor exists in that market:**
- Use relatable comparisons ("like finding Mexican-flavored snacks in North America")
- Highlight how Asian markets approach flavors differently
- Position cross-cultural products as legitimate creations

### Product Differentiation Section
**For Chips/Snacks:**
- Explain oil differences (palm oil = crispier, lighter texture)
- Describe texture differences from US versions
- Note recipe variations

**For Beverages:**
- Explain fruit variety differences (white peach vs regular peach)
- Note sweetness/flavor differences
- Mention regional preferences

### Product Details List
Standard items:
- Flavor: [Name] - [brief description]
- Size: [Imperial] ([Metric]) [package type]
- Made in [Country] for [market]
- Key differentiators
- "Limited import availability"

## HTML Formatting

### Spacing
- Add `<br>` tags between paragraphs
- Use `<br>` before section headers
- Keep Product Details as a `<ul>` list

### Template:
```html
<p>Opening hook with <strong>key feature</strong></p>
<br>
<p><em>Cultural Context Header:</em> Explanation...</p>
<br>
<p><strong>Differentiation Header:</strong> Details...</p>
<br>
<p>Flavor description...</p>
<br>
<p><strong>Product Details:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Detail 1</li>
<li>Detail 2</li>
</ul>
```

## Key Selling Points

### Exclusivity
- "Never released in the US"
- "Exclusive to Asian markets"
- "Limited import availability"
- "Impossible to find in American stores"

### Authenticity
- Mention real ingredients when known
- Reference original country production
- Note collector appeal
- Highlight limited edition status

### Experience
- Position as adventure/discovery
- Appeal to "international snack enthusiasts"
- Mention sharing with friends
- Reference "cult following" when applicable

## Special Considerations

### Chinese Products
Add disclaimer: "*Note: Chinese beverages display production dates rather than expiration dates. Check the label for shelf life information (typically 12 months from production).*"

### Chips/Fried Snacks
- Always mention oil type if different from US standard
- Palm oil = crispier, lighter texture
- Explain how this affects eating experience

### Unfamiliar Flavors
- Take time to explain
- Use American flavor references as anchors
- Example: "White peach is like regular peach but sweeter, more fragrant, less acidic"

## Tone Guidelines

- **Be enthusiastic** but not overly salesy
- **Be educational** - customers want to understand what they're buying
- **Be specific** about flavors and textures
- **Be honest** about differences from American products
- **Be inviting** - make customers feel like they're discovering something special
