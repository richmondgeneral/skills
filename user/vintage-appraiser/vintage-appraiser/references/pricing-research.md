# Pricing Research Workflow

## Overview

Arrr, so ye want to know what the treasure be worth! Pricin' antiques and vintage goods ain't an exact science, savvy? It be more like readin' the tides—ye need multiple data points to chart yer course true.

## The Golden Rule of Pricing

**Price = What a willing buyer paid a willing seller, recently, for a comparable item.**

Not what someone's *asking*. Not what Grandma *said* it was worth. What actually *sold*.

## Pricing Research Workflow

### Step 1: Establish the Item Identity

Before pricin', nail down exactly what ye have:

| Factor | Why It Matters |
|--------|----------------|
| Maker/Manufacturer | Same pattern, different maker = different value |
| Pattern/Model | Specific identification, not generic description |
| Age/Era | Original vs reproduction, early vs late production |
| Size/Form | A 10" bowl vs 6" bowl be different markets |
| Color/Variant | Rare colors command premiums |
| Condition | The barnacle on every ship's hull |

### Step 2: Search Sold Comparables

**Tier 1 Sources (Most Reliable):**

| Source | Access | Best For |
|--------|--------|----------|
| **eBay Sold Listings** | Free (filter: "Sold Items") | Volume, recent sales, common items |
| **LiveAuctioneers** | Free (past results) | Mid-to-high value, specialty items |
| **Worthpoint** | Subscription (~$30/mo) | Deep archive, pattern matching |
| **Invaluable** | Free (past results) | Auction house aggregation |

**Tier 2 Sources (Supplemental):**

| Source | Access | Best For |
|--------|--------|----------|
| **Ruby Lane** | Free (browse) | Dealer pricing (asking, not sold) |
| **1stDibs** | Free (browse) | High-end dealer pricing |
| **Etsy Sold** | Limited visibility | Vintage/craft crossover |
| **Auction house archives** | Varies | Important pieces, provenance |

### Step 3: Search Strategy

**Effective search terms:**

```
[Maker] [Pattern] [Form] [Size if relevant]

Examples:
- "Northwood Grape Cable bowl marigold"
- "Roseville Pinecone vase 8 inch"
- "Depression glass cherry blossom pitcher pink"
```

**Refinin' yer search:**

- Too many results? Add color, size, or form
- Too few results? Remove modifiers, try pattern only
- No results? Search broader category, check spelling variations
- Still nothing? Item may be rare OR misidentified

### Step 4: Analyze the Comparables

**Minimum sample:** 3-5 sold examples (more for common items)

**Record for each comparable:**

| Data Point | Why |
|------------|-----|
| Sale price | The anchor |
| Sale date | Recent = relevant (prefer < 12 months) |
| Venue | eBay vs auction house affects price |
| Condition noted | Adjust for condition differences |
| Any damage/repairs | Significant price factor |

### Step 5: Adjust for Variables

**Condition adjustments** (rough guidelines from mint):

| Condition | Typical Adjustment |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Mint/Excellent | Baseline (100%) |
| Very Good (minor wear) | 70-85% |
| Good (visible wear, no damage) | 50-70% |
| Fair (chips, cracks, repairs) | 20-40% |
| Poor (major damage) | Decorative value only |

**Venue adjustments:**

| Selling Venue | Price Expectation |
|---------------|-------------------|
| Auction house (major) | Highest for quality pieces |
| eBay auction | Market rate baseline |
| eBay Buy It Now | 10-20% premium over auction |
| Antique mall/shop | 30-50% over eBay (overhead) |
| Estate sale | 50-70% of retail |

**Timing adjustments:**

- Holiday/gift seasons: +10-20% for decorative items
- Summer slowdown: -10-20% typical
- Post-estate-sale flood: Prices temporarily down for that maker

### Step 6: Synthesize a Price Range

**Output format:**

```
Estimated Value Range: $XX - $XX

Based on:
- [X] comparable sales from [sources]
- Sale dates: [date range]
- Condition adjustment: [if applicable]

Recent comparable examples:
1. [Item description] - $XX ([venue], [date])
2. [Item description] - $XX ([venue], [date])
3. [Item description] - $XX ([venue], [date])

Notes: [Any relevant factors affecting value]
```

## Special Considerations by Category

### Carnival Glass Pricing

**Value hierarchy:**
1. Rare patterns (Farmyard, People's Vase, etc.) - $1,000s-$10,000s
2. Rare colors in common patterns - 5-50x common color
3. Rare forms (plates vs bowls) - 2-5x common form
4. Common patterns in common colors - $20-$100 typically

**Carnival-specific factors:**
- Iridescence quality: Strong multicolor > pale marigold
- Edge treatment: Ruffled, candy ribbon, 3-in-1 edge all affect value
- Base: Collar base, spatula foot noted separately
- Radium finish (Millersburg): Premium

### Pottery Pricing

**Value factors:**
- Artist signature (for art pottery): 2-10x unsigned
- Glaze quality/rare glazes: Significant premiums
- Size: Larger generally more valuable
- Form: Vases > bowls > utilitarian

### Silver Pricing

**Dual value consideration:**
- Melt value (weight × spot price) = floor
- Collectible premium = maker, pattern, rarity
- Some pieces: Melt > collectible (common patterns)
- Important makers: Collectible >> melt

### Furniture Pricing

**The local factor:**
- Furniture = expensive to ship
- Local market matters more than national
- Brown furniture currently depressed
- Mid-century modern: Strong national market

## Red Flags in Pricing Research

**Watch for:**
- Shill bidding (auction prices artificially high)
- Dealer asking prices (not actual sales)
- "Make offer" listings (final price unknown)
- Outlier sales (unsold relists, special circumstances)
- International sales (VAT, different markets)

**When data conflicts:**
- Weight recent sales heavier
- Prefer auction results to fixed price
- Consider condition carefully in each sale
- More data points = more reliable range

## Quick Reference: Margin Targets by Venue

When buyin' to resell, ye need room for profit, aye?

| Selling Venue | Target Buy Price |
|---------------|------------------|
| eBay/Online | 30-40% of expected sale |
| Antique mall | 40-50% of expected sale |
| Show/Market | 30-50% of expected sale |
| Auction consignment | 50-60% of expected hammer |

**The Rule of Three:** If ye can't triple yer money (or at least double), the treasure ain't worth haulin' aboard—unless it be fer yer own collection, matey!
