# Research Sources for Maker's Mark Identification

## Online Databases by Category

### Ceramics & Pottery

| Resource | Coverage | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Kovels (kovels.com) | American & European | Subscription; extensive mark database |
| The Marks Project (themarksproject.org) | American art pottery | Free; photos of actual marks |
| Gotheborg (gotheborg.com) | Asian ceramics | Free; excellent for Chinese/Japanese |
| Replacements Ltd | Dinnerware patterns | Free; good for tableware ID |

**Search strategy for pottery**: "[visible text] pottery mark" or "[symbol description] ceramics mark [country if known]"

### Glass

| Resource | Coverage | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Glass Encyclopaedia (glass-encyclopaedia.com) | European art glass | Free; maker histories |
| Carnival Glass Worldwide (carnivalglassworldwide.com) | Carnival glass | Free; patterns and makers |
| EAPG (Early American Pattern Glass) databases | Pressed glass 1850-1910 | Various collector sites |
| Fenton Art Glass Collectors | Fenton-specific | Mark variations by era |

**Carnival glass specifics**: Base marks less common than pattern ID. Focus on pattern name + base shape + color. Northwood "N" in circle is most recognized mark.

### Silver & Metalware

| Resource | Coverage | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| 925-1000 (925-1000.com) | Silver hallmarks worldwide | Free; searchable by symbol |
| Online Encyclopedia of Silver Marks | American makers | Free; extensive |
| Encyclopaedia of Silver (encyclopediaofsilvermarks.com) | British hallmarks | Includes date letter charts |

**British hallmark system** (read left to right):
1. Maker's mark (initials)
2. Standard mark (lion passant = sterling)
3. Assay office mark (leopard = London, anchor = Birmingham, etc.)
4. Date letter (changes yearly, style indicates cycle)
5. Optional duty mark (monarch's head, 1784-1890)

### Furniture

| Resource | Coverage | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Furniture City History (various) | Grand Rapids makers | Regional focus |
| FADA (Furniture Archive Database) | American furniture | Limited but growing |

**Furniture dating approach**: Marks less standardized than other categories. Focus on construction methods, hardware, wood species alongside any stamps/labels.

### Jewelry

| Resource | Coverage | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Langantiques Encyclopaedia | Costume jewelry | Maker histories |
| RAOV (Rhinestones and Old Vintage) | Costume jewelry marks | Collector community |

## Auction Archives (Excellent for Comparables)

- **LiveAuctioneers** - Search completed auctions; see how similar marks were attributed
- **Worthpoint** - Subscription; massive sold-item database
- **eBay Sold Listings** - Free; search "sold" filter for attribution examples
- **Invaluable** - Auction house results aggregator

## Research Tips

**When web search fails:**
1. Try image search with photo of actual mark
2. Search for the piece type + "unmarked" to find attribution discussions
3. Check collector forums (often have active ID threads)
4. Look for pattern/shape identification first, then narrow to maker

**Red flags for fakes/reproductions:**
- Marks that are too crisp/perfect on "old" pieces
- Modern mark styles on antique forms
- "Made in [Country]" on pieces styled pre-1914
- Inconsistent wear (mark pristine, piece worn)

**When confidence is low:**
- Acknowledge uncertainty clearly
- Provide the research path taken
- Suggest specialist consultation for valuable pieces
- Auction house specialists often provide free verbal opinions
